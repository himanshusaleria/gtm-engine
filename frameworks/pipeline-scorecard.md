# Pipeline Scorecard — Track prospect commitment with actions, not words

**Attribution:** Commitment Scorecard framework

## What Is It?

A structured scoring system that evaluates how committed each prospect in your pipeline actually is. Instead of relying on gut feeling ("this deal feels good"), you score prospects across multiple dimensions — and critically, you separate **what they say** from **what they do**.

The scorecard answers: "Which deals in my pipeline are real, and which am I fooling myself about?"

## Why It Matters for 0-1 Sales

At the 0-1 stage, every deal in your pipeline feels precious. You've only got 5-10 prospects, so you want to believe all of them will close. This creates two dangerous traps:

1. **Happy ears** — interpreting polite interest as buying intent
2. **Even distribution of effort** — spending equal time on all deals instead of focusing on the ones most likely to close

The scorecard forces objectivity. When you score a deal and it comes back as a 4 out of 12, you know to either escalate your approach or move on — no matter how "excited" the prospect sounded on the last call.

## The Framework

### Four Scoring Dimensions

| Dimension | What It Measures | Key Question |
|-----------|-----------------|--------------|
| **Engagement** | How actively they participate | Are they showing up, responding, and investing time? |
| **Problem Acknowledgment** | How clearly they own the problem | Have they articulated the pain in their own words? |
| **Timeline** | How urgent the resolution is | Is there a forcing function or deadline? |
| **Resource Commitment** | How much they've invested in the evaluation | Have they committed time, people, data, or budget? |

### Words vs Actions

This is the most important distinction in the scorecard. Everyone can say nice things. Actions reveal truth.

| Dimension | Words (what they say) | Actions (what they do) |
|-----------|----------------------|----------------------|
| **Engagement** | "Let's definitely keep talking" | Responds within 24 hours, attends every meeting, brings colleagues |
| **Problem Acknowledgment** | "Yeah, that's a problem for us" | Shares specific data about the problem, quantifies the cost |
| **Timeline** | "We want to solve this soon" | Gives a specific date, ties it to a business event (board meeting, launch, audit) |
| **Resource Commitment** | "I'll check with my team" | Introduces you to the decision-maker, shares system access, allocates evaluation time |

### Scoring Scale

Each dimension is scored 1-3:

| Score | Level | Definition |
|-------|-------|-----------|
| **1** | No signal | No evidence of commitment in this dimension |
| **2** | Verbal / Partial | They've said the right things but haven't backed it with actions |
| **3** | Demonstrated action | They've taken concrete, observable action that proves commitment |

### Pipeline Health Bands

| Total Score | Health | What It Means | Your Play |
|------------|--------|--------------|-----------|
| **10-12** | Strong | High commitment, likely to close | Push for next steps, remove friction, accelerate timeline |
| **7-9** | Promising | Good signals but gaps remain | Identify the low-scoring dimension and address it directly |
| **5-6** | Developing | Some interest but not enough commitment | Qualify harder — are they real or just being polite? |
| **Below 5** | At Risk | Little evidence of real intent | Have an honest conversation or deprioritize |

## How to Apply It

### Setting Up Your Scorecard

1. **List all active prospects** in your pipeline
2. **Score each dimension** using the 1-3 scale
3. **Be honest** — score based on evidence, not hope
4. **Note the evidence** for each score (what specifically did they do?)

### Using the Scorecard Weekly

Every week:
1. **Rescore** each prospect based on new information
2. **Look for movement** — did any scores change? Up or down?
3. **Identify gaps** — which dimension is lowest for your strongest deals?
4. **Plan actions** — for each deal, what would move the lowest dimension up by 1 point?

### Scoring Triggers

Use these concrete examples to calibrate your scoring:

**Engagement:**
- 1: Ghosting, delayed responses, canceling meetings
- 2: Responds to emails, attends scheduled calls, says "interested"
- 3: Responds same-day, proactively schedules follow-ups, brings additional stakeholders

**Problem Acknowledgment:**
- 1: Hasn't described the problem in their own words
- 2: Says "yeah, that's a challenge for us" but no specifics
- 3: Shares data, tells you how much it costs them, has tried to solve it before

**Timeline:**
- 1: "No rush" / "Maybe next quarter" / no date mentioned
- 2: "We'd like to solve this in the next few months"
- 3: "We need this before [specific date] because [specific reason]" — board meeting, compliance deadline, product launch

**Resource Commitment:**
- 1: Only talking to you, no internal actions taken
- 2: "I'll talk to my boss" or "Let me share this with the team"
- 3: Introduced you to the decision-maker, allocated team members for evaluation, shared system access or data, discussed budget

## Example

**Scenario:** You sell a B2B data integration platform. Here are three prospects in your pipeline.

### Prospect: Acme Corp (Series B, 80 engineers)

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Engagement | 3 | CTO attended both calls, brought their data lead to the second call, responded to follow-up within 2 hours |
| Problem Ack. | 3 | Shared that they spend 20 engineering hours/week on custom data pipelines, showed their internal tracking spreadsheet |
| Timeline | 2 | Said "we want to solve this in Q2" but no specific forcing function |
| Resources | 2 | CTO is engaged but hasn't introduced procurement or discussed budget |
| **Total** | **10** | **Strong** |

**Action:** Push for a specific timeline trigger ("Is there a product launch or board milestone driving the Q2 target?") and ask to meet the budget holder.

### Prospect: Beta Inc (Seed stage, 15 engineers)

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Engagement | 2 | Founder took one call, said "let's reconnect in a few weeks," hasn't responded to follow-up |
| Problem Ack. | 1 | Said "yeah, data stuff is messy" but no specifics, no quantification |
| Timeline | 1 | No timeline mentioned |
| Resources | 1 | Just the founder, no other stakeholders involved |
| **Total** | **5** | **At Risk** |

**Action:** Send one more follow-up with a specific insight about their situation. If no response, deprioritize and move to nurture.

### Prospect: Gamma Labs (Series A, 40 engineers)

| Dimension | Score | Evidence |
|-----------|-------|---------|
| Engagement | 2 | VP Eng attended demo, asked good questions, but took 5 days to respond to proposal |
| Problem Ack. | 3 | Shared that a broken data pipeline caused a customer-facing outage last month, showed the postmortem |
| Timeline | 3 | "We need to fix this before our enterprise launch in 8 weeks" |
| Resources | 1 | VP Eng is the only contact, says "I need to get buy-in from the CEO" |
| **Total** | **9** | **Promising** |

**Action:** The gap is Resources. Ask: "Would it help if I put together a one-pager for your CEO? And could we do a 15-minute call with them to address their questions directly?"

## Connection to Other Frameworks

- **Customer Profile (ICP):** Only score prospects that pass your ICP filter. A high commitment score on a non-ICP account is a distraction.
- **Discovery Questions:** Good discovery calls produce the data you need to score accurately. If you can't score a dimension, you need a better discovery question for it.
- **Intent Signals:** Inbound prospects from strong intent signals often start with higher scores. Track whether signal strength predicts final commitment.
- **Messaging Structure:** For "Developing" deals, use outcome messaging to re-engage — remind them of the impact and outcome.
- **Market Segments (Buyer's Pyramid):** Prospects from the 3% "buying now" tier should score higher on Timeline. If they don't, something is wrong.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | What Happens | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **Scoring on enthusiasm** | Prospect was excited on the call, so you give them all 3s | Score on ACTIONS, not WORDS. Excitement without follow-through is a 2, not a 3. |
| **Never rescoring** | You scored once and never updated | Rescore weekly. Scores should move — if they don't, the deal is stalling. |
| **Ignoring low scores** | A deal scores 5/12 but you keep working it because "it could turn around" | Set a rule: below 5 for two consecutive weeks = deprioritize or have a direct conversation |
| **No evidence for scores** | You give a 3 but can't point to a specific action | Every score of 2 or 3 must have a written justification. If you can't write it, lower the score. |
| **All deals score the same** | Your pipeline has five prospects all scored 7/12 | Either you're not being granular enough, or you need better discovery to differentiate |
| **Not acting on gaps** | You see that Timeline is a 1 but don't ask about deadlines | Each low score is a to-do: craft a question or action that would surface the missing information |
