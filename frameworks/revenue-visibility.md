# Revenue Visibility — One Sheet Between You and Cash-Flow Panic

A single operating sheet that tracks every revenue opportunity, scores how likely the money is to actually arrive, and ties every action you take this week to raising that likelihood.

## What Is It?

Revenue visibility is a cash-flow management practice built on three questions:

- **Clarity:** Do you know, today, where your month stands against your revenue target?
- **Visibility:** Of the revenue you've targeted or proposed, how much is actually likely to convert — based on evidence?
- **Predictability:** Given the actions you're taking, can you predict whether revenue is on track and when it hits your bank?

The tool is one spreadsheet: **one row per opportunity**, a 1-5 visibility score per row driven by *buyer actions* (not buyer words), and a named next action for every row that isn't yet money in the bank.

## Why It Matters for 0-1 Sales

Founders doing first-time sales live in one of two states: irrational optimism ("we have $200K in the pipeline!") or 3am panic ("is ANY of this actually closing?"). Both come from the same root cause — no honest, evidence-based view of which revenue is real.

The visibility sheet fixes three founder failure modes:

- **Confusing pipeline with revenue.** A proposal sent is not money. The sheet forces you to score each deal on what the buyer has *done*, not what they've said.
- **Busy-ness without progress.** If an action you're planning doesn't raise the visibility score of any row on the sheet, question whether you should be doing it. This one rule kills most founder busywork.
- **Surprise misses.** When you target 100K and land 70K, either your assumptions were wrong or something changed with a customer. The sheet makes which one visible — row by row — so next month's target is smarter.

## The Framework

### The sheet

One row per opportunity, with columns:

| Column | What it means |
|--------|---------------|
| **Opportunity** | Company / deal name |
| **Offering** | What you're selling them (multi-product deals get one row per offering) |
| **Revenue target** | Total expected from this opportunity |
| **Confirmed** | Revenue contracted / committed |
| **Unconfirmed** | Revenue expected but not yet committed — this is your visibility gap |
| **Visibility score (1-5)** | How confident you are the unconfirmed money arrives (scoring below) |
| **Visibility-raising action** | The specific next move to raise this row's score |
| **Invoiced / On track / In bank** | The mechanical trail from commitment to cash |
| **Remarks** | Blockers, context |

### The visibility score

The score reflects evidence, and the evidence is **buyer actions**:

| Score | Meaning | Evidence looks like |
|-------|---------|---------------------|
| **1** | Just entered the funnel | First meeting done. Prospect exploring. Words only. |
| **2** | Some signals | Proposal sent and opened; follow-up questions asked; meetings accepted. |
| **3** | Actions starting | Prospect is investing effort — sharing data, connecting team members, scheduling follow-ups. Words AND actions align. |
| **4** | Deal is moving | Budget discussed, internal champion active, timeline agreed. Actions dominate. |
| **5** | Money is coming | Terms agreed, invoice ready or sent. Waiting on payment mechanics only. |

Two rules that keep the score honest:

1. **Words never move a score past 2.** Only actions do. "We're very excited" with no effort invested is a 1-2, however warm it felt.
2. **Scores go down as well as up.** A prospect at 3 who goes silent for two weeks is a 2. Re-score weekly; don't let rows fossilize at their high-water mark.

### Visibility-raising actions

Every unconfirmed row needs a named action, and the actions come from your other frameworks:

| Action type | Example |
|-------------|---------|
| Deepen discovery | "Run a consequence-question session with the ops lead — pain isn't quantified yet" |
| Elevate commitment | "Ask them to invest something: staging access, a data sample, a stakeholder intro" |
| Fix positioning | "Stop sending feature material; re-anchor on the cost they named in call 2" |
| Close the words-actions gap | "They said they'd send the dataset — chase it; if it doesn't come, score drops to 2" |

## How to Apply It

### Setup (start of month)
1. List every opportunity — existing customers AND pipeline. If it enters the top of the funnel, it goes on the sheet, even if the value is a guess.
2. Set the revenue split: confirmed vs unconfirmed.
3. Score visibility 1-5 on today's evidence.
4. Write one visibility-raising action per unconfirmed row.

### Weekly review (15 minutes)
1. Re-score every row. What moved? What didn't?
2. For each action you took: did it raise a score? If nothing you did this week touched the sheet, that's the finding.
3. Any score that hasn't moved in 2 weeks → the deal is stalling. Diagnose (wrong stakeholder? unquantified pain? words-actions gap?) and change the action.
4. Any score that dropped → write the recovery action now, not next week.

### Monthly review
1. Target vs achieved — and per-row, why.
2. Pattern-hunt: "deals of type X always stall at 3" is a repeatable insight about your process, not bad luck.
3. Set next month's target from the sheet's evidence, not from hope.

## Workshop: Build Yours

1. Open a blank sheet and list every current opportunity — aim for completeness over accuracy.
2. Score each 1-5 using the action-evidence table. Be brutal: words cap at 2.
3. Sum the revenue at score 4-5. That's your *real* pipeline. Compare it to the number you've been telling yourself.
4. For every row at 1-3: write one visibility-raising action, with a date.
5. Put the weekly review in your calendar. The sheet only works as a living document.

## Example: The Three-Stage Deal

A founder selling to a logistics company structures a complex deal as three rows of the same opportunity:

| Opportunity | Offering | Target | Visibility | Action |
|-------------|----------|--------|------------|--------|
| Logistics Co. | Entry product (tracking module) | $500/mo | 5 — contract signed, trial live | Invoice |
| Logistics Co. | Diagnostic study (upsell) | $6K one-time | 2 — not yet proposed | Present findings from the trial as the bridge |
| Logistics Co. | Full platform | $35K/yr | 1 — future, depends on study | None yet — raising row 2 raises row 3 |

The insight: the entry product is both standalone revenue AND the evidence-generator for the next stage. Each stage raises visibility for the one after it. Without the sheet, this reads as "one deal, going fine." With it, you can see exactly which revenue is real (the $500/mo) and which needs work (everything else).

- **[Pipeline Scorecard](pipeline-scorecard.md)** — The visibility score is the revenue-facing twin of the commitment scorecard. Words-vs-actions evidence from the scorecard drives the 1-5 visibility score.
- **[Consequence Questions](consequence-questions.md)** — A deal stuck at visibility 2 usually means pain was never quantified — you pitched before you understood. Consequence-based discovery is the standard recovery action.
- **[Positioning Levels](positioning-levels.md)** — Rows that stall at 2-3 are often Vitamin-positioned deals. The visibility-raising action is repositioning, not another follow-up email.
- **[Sales Playbook](sales-playbook.md)** — Each visibility band maps loosely to playbook stages; a row whose score outruns its completed stages (excited buyer, skipped discovery) is a deal built on sand.

## Anti-Patterns

### Scoring on words
"They LOVED the demo" is not evidence. What did they *do* after the demo? If the answer is nothing, the score is unchanged — or lower.

### Only tracking new pipeline
Existing-customer revenue belongs on the sheet too. Renewal risk and expansion opportunities have visibility scores just like new deals — and founders forget them until the churn email arrives.

### Actions that don't map to rows
A week of "sales work" — content, events, tool-tinkering — that raises no row's score is a week of pipeline theater. The sheet is the filter: no row, no action.

### Letting scores only go up
A sheet where scores never drop is a mood board, not an instrument. Silence, delays, and unreturned commitments all move scores DOWN. Re-score honestly every week.

### Waiting for "clarity" to add a row
You don't need a precise deal value to add an opportunity. Put it on the sheet the moment it enters the funnel; refine the numbers as visibility rises. An incomplete sheet you use daily beats a perfect one you don't.
