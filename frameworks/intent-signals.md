# Intent Signals — Diagnose buyer interest before you sell

**Attribution:** Intent Diagnostic framework — six methods for reading buyer readiness

## What Is It?

A systematic approach to identifying and scoring signals that indicate a prospect is ready (or getting ready) to buy. Instead of cold-calling into the void, you observe what prospects **do** and use those behaviors to prioritize outreach and tailor your message.

Intent signals answer the question: "Is this prospect moving toward a purchase, or am I interrupting someone who doesn't care?"

## Why It Matters for 0-1 Sales

Early-stage founders don't have the luxury of high-volume outreach. Every email, every call, every demo costs precious time. Intent signals help you:

- **Prioritize ruthlessly** — reach out to prospects showing buying behavior first
- **Time your outreach** — arrive when the problem is top-of-mind, not when it's been shelved
- **Personalize at scale** — reference the specific signal in your message ("I noticed you're hiring for X...")
- **Shorten sales cycles** — prospects with strong intent signals close faster because the problem is already real to them

## The Framework

### Six Signal Types

| Signal Type | What to Look For | Where to Find It |
|-------------|-----------------|-----------------|
| **1. Hiring Signals** | Job postings for roles related to the problem you solve | LinkedIn Jobs, Indeed, company careers page |
| **2. Tech Stack Signals** | Adoption or removal of adjacent tools | BuiltWith, Wappalyzer, job descriptions mentioning specific tech |
| **3. Content Consumption** | Engaging with content about the problem space | Blog subscribers, webinar attendees, whitepaper downloads, social engagement |
| **4. Event Attendance** | Showing up at relevant industry events or communities | Conference attendee lists, Slack communities, meetup RSVPs |
| **5. Competitive Evaluation** | Actively comparing solutions in your category | G2/Capterra reviews, comparison page visits, competitor mentions |
| **6. Pain Triggers** | External events that create or amplify the problem | Incidents, outages, regulatory changes, leadership changes, rapid growth, complaints on social media |

### Three Stages of Buyer Readiness

| Stage | Behavior | What It Means | Your Play |
|-------|----------|--------------|-----------|
| **Stage 1 — Passive** | Reading blog posts, following thought leaders, lurking in communities | Aware of the problem space but not actively solving | Educational content, thought leadership, stay visible |
| **Stage 2 — Active** | Comparing solutions, attending demos, asking peers for recommendations | Actively evaluating options, building a shortlist | Direct outreach, case studies, offer a conversation |
| **Stage 3 — Urgent** | Hiring for the role, allocating budget, building internally, had a recent incident | Must solve this NOW — timeline is compressed | Fast response, streamlined evaluation, remove friction |

### Signal Scoring

Not all signals are equal. Score them by how directly they indicate purchase intent:

| Strength | Definition | Examples |
|----------|-----------|----------|
| **Strong** | Directly indicates buying behavior or urgent need | Hiring for the exact role your product replaces, active RFP, competitor contract expiring, recent incident |
| **Medium** | Adjacent indicator — suggests the problem is real but not urgent | Adopting complementary tools, attending industry events, engaging with competitor content |
| **Weak** | General interest — could mean many things | Following your company on LinkedIn, reading a blog post, generic job posting |

### Signal Combinations

Single signals can be misleading. Combinations are powerful:

| Combination | What It Suggests |
|-------------|-----------------|
| Hiring + Tech stack change | Building a new function — high intent |
| Content consumption + Event attendance | Educating themselves — Stage 2 |
| Pain trigger + Competitive evaluation | Urgent and shopping — Stage 3 |
| Hiring + Pain trigger | Just experienced the problem AND investing in solving it — highest intent |

## How to Apply It

### Step 1: Define Your Signal Map

For your specific product, which signals matter most? Map each signal type to concrete, observable indicators.

Example for a DevOps monitoring tool:

| Signal Type | Concrete Indicator |
|-------------|-------------------|
| Hiring | SRE, DevOps, Platform Engineer job posts |
| Tech Stack | Adopting Kubernetes, migrating to cloud |
| Content | Reading "observability" blog posts, attending SREcon |
| Event | Participating in incident management communities |
| Competitive | Reviewing Datadog/New Relic on G2 |
| Pain Trigger | Public outage, postmortem published, angry tweets about downtime |

### Step 2: Build Signal Detection Routines

Set up weekly or daily checks:

- **LinkedIn:** Search for job postings with relevant keywords in your ICP companies
- **Google Alerts:** Set alerts for company names + problem keywords
- **G2/Capterra:** Monitor new reviews in your category
- **Social media:** Track relevant hashtags or community discussions
- **News:** Watch for funding rounds, leadership changes, incidents

### Step 3: Score and Prioritize

When you detect a signal:

1. **Score it:** Strong, Medium, or Weak
2. **Check ICP fit:** Does the company match your customer profile?
3. **Check for combinations:** Are there multiple signals stacking up?
4. **Prioritize:** Strong signal + ICP fit + combination = top of your outreach list

### Step 4: Reference the Signal in Outreach

Never waste a signal by sending generic outreach. Mention it:

> "I noticed your team posted an SRE role last week — we've been working with similar teams who found that [specific outcome]. Would it be worth a 15-minute conversation?"

## Example

**Scenario:** You sell a B2B tool for automated API testing to engineering teams.

### Signal Map

| Signal Type | Indicator | Strength | Where to Find |
|-------------|-----------|----------|--------------|
| **Hiring** | "QA Engineer" or "SDET" job posting | Strong | LinkedIn Jobs |
| **Hiring** | "Backend Engineer" with "testing" in description | Medium | Job boards |
| **Tech Stack** | Adopted microservices architecture recently | Medium | Engineering blog, job descriptions |
| **Tech Stack** | Using Postman or manual API testing tools | Strong | BuiltWith, job descriptions |
| **Content** | CTO posts about "testing bottleneck" on LinkedIn | Strong | LinkedIn feed |
| **Content** | Engineers starring API testing repos on GitHub | Weak | GitHub activity |
| **Event** | Attending QA conferences or testing meetups | Medium | Event attendee lists |
| **Competitive** | Reviewing API testing tools on G2 | Strong | G2 category page |
| **Pain Trigger** | Public API outage or breaking change | Strong | Status pages, Hacker News, Twitter |
| **Pain Trigger** | Rapid team growth (2x engineers in 6 months) | Medium | LinkedIn headcount data |

### Outreach Using Signals

**Signal detected:** Company posted SDET role + had a public API incident last month.

> Subject: after the api incident
>
> Hey [Name],
>
> I noticed [Company] had an API issue last month and you're now hiring an SDET. Sounds like testing coverage is top of mind.
>
> We work with engineering teams at similar scale who were spending 40% of QA time on manual API testing. After automating their API test suite, they caught regression issues 3x faster.
>
> Worth a 15-min chat to see if it's relevant to what you're building out?

## Connection to Other Frameworks

- **Customer Profile (ICP):** Signals tell you WHEN to reach out. ICP tells you WHO to reach out to. A strong signal on a non-ICP account is still a waste of time.
- **Market Segments (Buyer's Pyramid):** Intent signals help you identify which pyramid tier a prospect sits in. Stage 1 = "not thinking about it." Stage 3 = "buying now."
- **Discovery Questions:** When a strong signal gets you a meeting, use Mom Test questions to validate whether the signal reflects a real, painful problem.
- **Messaging Structure:** The signal itself becomes the "Issue" in your outreach message — it's why you're reaching out.
- **Pipeline Scorecard:** Signal strength feeds into your commitment scoring. A prospect who came inbound after a pain trigger scores higher on urgency.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | What Happens | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **Signal hoarding** | You collect signals but never act on them | Set a rule: act on every strong signal within 48 hours |
| **Weak signal chasing** | You spend time on prospects who liked a LinkedIn post | Only prioritize strong and medium signals; weak signals go in a nurture list |
| **Signal without ICP check** | You reach out to every company with a job posting, regardless of fit | Always cross-check signals against your ICP before outreach |
| **Generic outreach on strong signals** | You detect a great signal but send a template email that doesn't mention it | Always reference the specific signal — it's your opening |
| **Stale signals** | You act on a job posting from 3 months ago | Set expiration: hiring signals valid for 30 days, pain triggers for 14 days |
| **Single-signal overconfidence** | One signal makes you treat a prospect as high-intent | Look for combinations — single signals are suggestive, combinations are compelling |
