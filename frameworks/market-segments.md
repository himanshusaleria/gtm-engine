# Market Segments — Know where your buyers actually sit

**Attribution:** Based on Chet Holmes' *The Ultimate Sales Machine*

## What Is It?

The Buyer's Pyramid is a model that segments any market by **purchase readiness**. It reveals that at any given moment, only a tiny fraction of your market is actively looking to buy. The rest — the vast majority — need to be educated, nurtured, or made aware before they'll ever consider your solution.

## Why It Matters for 0-1 Sales

Most early-stage founders only target the top of the pyramid: people actively searching for a solution. This creates two problems:

1. **Tiny addressable audience.** You're fighting over 3% of the market with every competitor, including incumbents with bigger budgets.
2. **Commoditized conversations.** When someone is already shopping, they compare features and price. You lose your ability to shape the conversation.

The real opportunity at 0-1 is the **30% "not thinking about it"** tier — people who have the problem but haven't prioritized solving it. If you can educate them and make the pain visible, you become the default solution before they ever start shopping.

## The Framework

### The Five Tiers

```
         /\
        /  \        3%  — Buying Now
       /    \           Actively searching, comparing vendors
      /------\
     /        \     7%  — Open To It
    /          \        Aware of the problem, considering solutions
   /------------\
  /              \  30% — Not Thinking About It
 /                \     Have the problem, haven't prioritized it
/------------------\
|                  | 30% — Think They're Not Interested
|                  |     Don't believe the problem applies to them
|__________________|
|                  | 30% — Know They're Not Interested
|__________________|     Genuinely not a fit right now
```

### Tier Breakdown

| Tier | % | Mindset | Your Approach |
|------|---|---------|--------------|
| **Buying Now** | 3% | "I need a solution this quarter" | Direct outreach, demos, proposals — but expect competition |
| **Open To It** | 7% | "I know this is a problem, I'd consider options" | Targeted content, case studies, warm intros |
| **Not Thinking About It** | 30% | "Yeah, we do that manually, it's fine" | Education-based outreach, thought leadership, events |
| **Think They're Not Interested** | 30% | "That's not really our problem" | Long-term brand building, reframing the problem |
| **Know They're Not Interested** | 30% | "We're genuinely not a fit" | Don't waste time here |

## How to Apply It

### Step 1: Map Your Current Outreach

Look at your last 20 outreach messages or calls. What tier are you targeting?

- If every message says "looking for a better way to do X?" — you're targeting the 3%
- If you lead with education ("here's what companies like yours are doing differently") — you're reaching the 30%

### Step 2: Build Tier-Specific Plays

**For the 3% (Buying Now):**
- Monitor job postings, RFP sites, G2/Capterra reviews
- Respond fast — speed wins at this tier
- Lead with ROI and competitive differentiation

**For the 7% (Open To It):**
- Share customer stories that mirror their situation
- Offer assessments or audits (low-commitment entry)
- Use warm intros from mutual connections

**For the 30% (Not Thinking About It) — your biggest opportunity:**
- Create content that names the problem they haven't articulated
- Run workshops or webinars that educate without selling
- Share data: "Companies your size typically waste X hours/month on this"
- Use "pattern interrupt" outreach that makes them rethink the status quo

### Step 3: Allocate Time by Tier

A common mistake is spending 80% of time on the 3% because those deals feel more "real." Instead:

| Tier | Time Allocation | Expected Conversion |
|------|----------------|-------------------|
| 3% Buying Now | 20% | High, but competitive |
| 7% Open To It | 30% | Medium, relationship-driven |
| 30% Not Thinking | 40% | Lower per-touch, but huge volume and less competition |
| Bottom 60% | 10% | Brand building only |

## Workshop: Build Yours

1. **Define your total market.** Who could theoretically use your product? (This is your whole pyramid.)
2. **Identify the 3%.** Where do people who are actively searching for your category show up? (Google, G2, job boards, Slack communities, Reddit?)
3. **Identify the 7%.** Who has acknowledged the problem but isn't urgently solving it? What signals tell you this? (Blog posts about the problem, conference talks, team complaints on social media?)
4. **Identify the 30% not thinking about it.** Who has the problem but doesn't realize it's costing them? What would make them realize? (Data, a benchmark, a competitor story?)
5. **Write one outreach message for each tier.** Notice how the tone shifts from "let me show you our solution" (3%) to "here's a pattern we're seeing" (30%).

## Example

**Scenario:** You sell a B2B SaaS platform for managing engineering on-call rotations.

| Tier | Who They Are | Signal | Your Play |
|------|-------------|--------|-----------|
| **3% Buying Now** | Teams actively evaluating PagerDuty alternatives | G2 comparison searches, "on-call tool" job requirements | Direct outreach: "I saw you're evaluating on-call tools — here's how we compare" |
| **7% Open To It** | Teams that had a major incident last quarter | Postmortem blog posts, Hacker News comments about on-call burnout | Case study: "How [similar company] reduced MTTR by 40%" |
| **30% Not Thinking** | Teams doing on-call via a shared Google Sheet | No public signals, but common at 20-50 person eng teams | Educational content: "The hidden cost of manual on-call: why eng teams lose 8 hours/week to scheduling" |
| **30% Think Not Interested** | Teams where the CTO thinks on-call is "fine" | No intent signals | Long-form content about on-call best practices — plant seeds |
| **30% Not Interested** | Solo founders, pre-product teams | Too early for this problem | Ignore for now |

## Connection to Other Frameworks

- **Customer Profile (ICP):** Your ICP defines WHO you target. The pyramid tells you WHERE they sit in terms of readiness. A perfect ICP match in the "not thinking about it" tier needs education, not a demo.
- **Intent Signals:** Intent diagnostic methods help you identify which tier a prospect is in based on their observable behavior.
- **Messaging Structure:** Your messaging tone shifts dramatically by tier — the 3% wants ROI comparisons, the 30% wants education.
- **Discovery Questions:** When you get a meeting with someone from the 30%, use Mom Test questions to understand if they have latent pain.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | What Happens | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **Only fishing in the 3%** | You compete on features/price with incumbents and lose | Build a content and outreach engine for the 30% |
| **Treating all tiers the same** | You send demo requests to people who don't know they have a problem | Segment your outreach by readiness tier |
| **Ignoring the 30% because it's "slow"** | You miss the largest, least-competitive segment | Dedicate 40% of outreach time to education-based plays |
| **Confusing "not interested" with "not thinking about it"** | You give up on prospects who just need to be educated | Test with 2-3 educational touches before writing someone off |
| **No patience for the middle tiers** | You want instant pipeline, so you only chase active buyers | Track "tier movement" — how many prospects moved up the pyramid this month? |
