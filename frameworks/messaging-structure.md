# Messaging Structure — Issue, Impact, Current Resolution, Outcome

**Attribution:** Outcome Messaging framework

## What Is It?

A four-part structure for writing outreach messages that connect with buyers emotionally and logically. Instead of leading with features ("we do X"), you lead with the prospect's world ("here's what's costing you"):

1. **Issue** — The specific problem they face
2. **Impact** — What it costs them (time, money, risk, frustration)
3. **Current Resolution** — How they're handling it today (badly)
4. **Outcome** — What life looks like after solving it

This structure works for cold emails, LinkedIn messages, landing pages, sales decks, and even verbal pitches.

## Why It Matters for 0-1 Sales

At the 0-1 stage, nobody knows your product. Nobody cares about your features. The only thing that gets a stranger to respond to your email is: **"This person understands my problem."**

Outcome messaging forces you to:

- **Lead with empathy, not ego** — talk about their pain, not your tech
- **Create urgency** — quantify the impact so they can't ignore it
- **Differentiate without comparing** — you're not saying "we're better than X," you're saying "here's a better outcome"
- **Position as painkiller, not vitamin** — vitamins are nice-to-have, painkillers are urgent

## The Framework

### The Four Parts

| Part | Purpose | Question to Answer |
|------|---------|-------------------|
| **Issue** | Name the specific problem | What keeps this person up at night? |
| **Impact** | Quantify the cost of inaction | What does this problem cost in hours, dollars, risk, or reputation? |
| **Current Resolution** | Show you understand their workaround | How are they dealing with it today, and why is that insufficient? |
| **Outcome** | Paint the after picture | What does life look like when this is solved? |

### Vitamin vs Painkiller Positioning

| | Vitamin | Painkiller |
|---|---------|-----------|
| **Framing** | "Nice to have" | "Need to have" |
| **Urgency** | Low — can buy later | High — costs money every day they wait |
| **Budget** | Discretionary — first to be cut | Essential — gets approved even in downturns |
| **Message** | "Improve your workflow" | "Stop losing $X every month to broken processes" |
| **Buyer emotion** | Mild interest | Relief, urgency |

Always position as painkiller. Even if your product has "vitamin" features, find the pain angle.

### Persona-Specific Messaging

Different buyers in the same company care about different impacts:

| Persona | What They Care About | Impact Language |
|---------|---------------------|----------------|
| **Individual Contributor** | Daily frustration, manual toil, wasted time | "You spend 4 hours every week on..." |
| **Manager** | Team velocity, hiring/retention, project timelines | "Your team ships 30% slower because..." |
| **Director/VP** | Cost, risk, competitive position | "This gap costs your org $X per quarter in..." |
| **C-Suite** | Revenue, growth, strategic risk | "Companies that solve this grow 2x faster..." |

### Email Craft Rules

**Subject lines:**
- Short (3-6 words)
- Lowercase (feels personal, not promotional)
- Reference their situation, not your product
- Examples: "your api testing gap", "after the outage", "re: sre hiring"

**Body:**
- 4-6 sentences maximum
- Lead with pain, not features
- One specific data point or observation
- Soft CTA (question, not demand)
- No attachments, no links in first email

**CTA examples (soft):**
- "Worth a 15-min chat?"
- "Does this resonate with what you're seeing?"
- "Happy to share how [similar company] handled this — interested?"
- "Would it make sense to explore this?"

**CTA examples (too aggressive — avoid):**
- "Book a demo here: [link]"
- "Let's schedule a call this week"
- "Are you available Tuesday at 2pm?"

## How to Apply It

### Step 1: Research the Prospect

Before writing, answer:
- What's their role and what are they measured on?
- What signals suggest they have this problem? (See Intent Signals framework)
- What's their current likely workaround?

### Step 2: Draft Using the Structure

Write one sentence for each part:

1. **Issue:** "[Specific problem] is common at companies like [theirs]"
2. **Impact:** "It typically costs [quantified impact]"
3. **Current Resolution:** "Most teams handle it with [workaround], which [why it's insufficient]"
4. **Outcome:** "Teams that solve this see [specific outcome]"

### Step 3: Cut Ruthlessly

- Remove anything about your product's features
- Remove anything that sounds like marketing copy
- Remove anything you wouldn't say to a friend over coffee
- Target: 4-6 sentences total

### Step 4: Personalize the Opening

Add one sentence at the top that shows you did research:
- "I noticed you're hiring for [role]..."
- "Saw your team's post about [topic]..."
- "Congrats on the Series B..."

## Workshop: Build Yours

1. **Pick your top 2-3 personas** (the people you sell to most often)

2. **For each persona, fill in the structure:**

   **Persona 1: ___**
   - Issue: ___
   - Impact (in their language): ___
   - Current Resolution: ___
   - Outcome: ___

   **Persona 2: ___**
   - Issue: ___
   - Impact (in their language): ___
   - Current Resolution: ___
   - Outcome: ___

3. **Write a full email** for each persona using the structure (4-6 sentences)

4. **Test it:** Read the email aloud. Does it sound like something a knowledgeable peer would say, or does it sound like a sales email? Rewrite until it sounds human.

## Example

**Scenario:** You sell a monitoring/observability tool for cloud infrastructure.

### Message to Ops Lead (IC/Manager)

> Subject: after the deployment Friday
>
> Hey Jordan,
>
> I saw your team had a rough deployment last Friday — downtime posts are never fun. Most ops teams at your scale are stitching together 3-4 monitoring tools and still missing issues until customers report them.
>
> Teams that consolidate their observability typically cut MTTR by 40% and stop finding out about outages from angry Slack messages.
>
> Worth a quick chat about what you're seeing on your end?

**Breakdown:**
- **Issue:** Deployment caused downtime (referenced a specific event)
- **Impact:** Missing issues until customers report them (daily pain for ops)
- **Current Resolution:** Stitching together 3-4 tools (common workaround)
- **Outcome:** 40% faster MTTR, proactive detection

### Same Product, Different Persona: VP of Engineering

> Subject: observability costs at scale
>
> Hi Taylor,
>
> Engineering teams at [Company]'s stage typically spend 15-20% of ops time on monitoring tool maintenance — configuring alerts, correlating across dashboards, chasing false positives.
>
> That's 2-3 engineers' worth of output going to tooling overhead instead of product work. The teams we work with have consolidated that down to a single pane and freed up that capacity.
>
> Does this match what you're seeing at [Company]?

**Breakdown:**
- **Issue:** Monitoring tool maintenance overhead
- **Impact:** 15-20% of ops time, 2-3 engineers' worth of output (speaks to VP's cost concern)
- **Current Resolution:** Multiple dashboards, manual correlation
- **Outcome:** Single pane, freed capacity (speaks to VP's strategic concern)

## Connection to Other Frameworks

- **Discovery Questions:** The pain points you uncover in discovery become the "Issue" and "Impact" in your messages. Good discovery = good messaging.
- **Customer Profile (ICP):** Your IPP defines the personas you write for. Each persona gets different impact language.
- **Intent Signals:** The signal you detected becomes the personalization hook at the top of your message.
- **Market Segments (Buyer's Pyramid):** Your messaging tone shifts by pyramid tier — the 3% wants ROI comparisons, the 30% wants education about a problem they haven't named yet.
- **Pipeline Scorecard:** Track which messaging approaches generate responses — this feeds back into your outreach strategy.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | What Happens | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **Feature-first messaging** | "We offer real-time monitoring with 200+ integrations" — prospect doesn't care | Lead with pain. Features come in the demo, not the email. |
| **No specific impact** | "This will save you time" — too vague to create urgency | Quantify: hours, dollars, percentage, headcount |
| **Same message for all personas** | The CTO gets the same email as the DevOps engineer | Write persona-specific versions — different people care about different impacts |
| **Wall of text** | 3 paragraphs, 2 links, a case study PDF attached | 4-6 sentences. No attachments. One CTA. |
| **Aggressive CTA** | "Let's book a call Tuesday at 2pm" — too presumptuous for a cold email | Soft CTA: "Does this resonate?" or "Worth a quick chat?" |
| **Vitamin positioning** | "Improve your team's efficiency" — sounds optional | Painkiller: "Stop losing 15 hours/week to manual monitoring" — sounds urgent |
| **No personalization** | Generic email that could be sent to anyone | Reference one specific thing about their company or situation |
