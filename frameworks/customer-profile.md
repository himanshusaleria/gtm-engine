# Customer Profile — Define who you're selling to at three levels

**Attribution:** Three-layer ICP model (IMP / ICP / IPP)

## What Is It?

A structured approach to defining your ideal customer across three layers:

1. **IMP (Ideal Market Profile)** — the market segment you're targeting
2. **ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — the account characteristics that make a company a great fit
3. **IPP (Ideal Prospect Profile)** — the specific person within that account who owns the problem

Most founders stop at "we sell to mid-market SaaS companies." That's too vague to drive outreach, qualify leads, or focus your pipeline. This framework forces precision at each layer.

## Why It Matters for 0-1 Sales

At the 0-1 stage, you can't sell to everyone. You don't have the team, the brand, or the case studies to go broad. The ICP framework helps you:

- **Focus outreach** on accounts most likely to convert
- **Qualify faster** — know within 5 minutes if a prospect is worth pursuing
- **Build repeatable patterns** — when you close a deal, you can find 50 more like it
- **Say no confidently** — the hardest skill in early sales is walking away from bad-fit prospects

## The Framework

### Layer 1: IMP (Ideal Market Profile)

Market-level attributes that define your playing field.

| Attribute | Questions to Answer |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Industry** | Which verticals have the problem you solve? |
| **Geography** | Where can you sell and support effectively? |
| **Company Size** | Revenue range, employee count, team size |
| **Tech Stack** | What technology indicates they're a fit? |
| **Growth Stage** | Startup, scale-up, enterprise? |
| **Regulatory Environment** | Do compliance requirements create urgency? |

### Layer 2: ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)

Account-level attributes that separate great customers from mediocre ones.

| Attribute | Questions to Answer |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Revenue / Funding** | Can they afford your solution? |
| **Team Size** (in the relevant function) | Is the team big enough to feel the pain? |
| **Growth Signals** | Are they hiring, expanding, launching new products? |
| **Tech Maturity** | Are they sophisticated enough to adopt your tool? |
| **Problem Evidence** | Can you see external signs of the problem? |
| **Decision Structure** | Is there a clear buyer, or is it a committee? |

### Layer 3: IPP (Ideal Prospect Profile)

Person-level attributes of your champion and buyer.

| Attribute | Questions to Answer |
|-----------|-------------------|
| **Title / Role** | Who owns the problem day-to-day? |
| **Decision Authority** | Can they buy, or do they need approval? |
| **Pain Ownership** | Do they personally suffer from the problem? |
| **Technical Depth** | Can they evaluate your solution themselves? |
| **Career Incentive** | Does solving this help them get promoted? |
| **Communication Style** | How do they prefer to be reached? |

### Three Tiers

Not every prospect that matches your IMP is equally valuable. Segment into tiers:

| Tier | Fit Level | How to Treat |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Core** (score 5-6) | Perfect fit across all layers | Invest heavily — personalized outreach, custom demos, founder-led sales |
| **Broader** (score 3-4) | Good fit with some gaps | Standard outreach — templated but relevant, qualify harder in discovery |
| **Universe** (score 0-2) | Marginal fit | Deprioritize — inbound only, no proactive outreach |

### Scoring Method

Pick 6 criteria from your ICP/IPP layers. Score each 0 or 1 (binary — either they have it or they don't).

| Criterion | 0 = No | 1 = Yes |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Right industry vertical | | |
| Team size above threshold | | |
| Active growth signals | | |
| Problem evidence visible | | |
| Right buyer title accessible | | |
| No disqualifying factors | | |
| **Total** | | **/6** |

### No-Go Rules

Some attributes are automatic disqualifiers regardless of total score:

- Company too small to have the problem (e.g., under 5 engineers for a dev tools product)
- No budget authority (e.g., individual contributor with no purchasing power)
- Competitor's customer under long-term contract
- Geographic or regulatory blocker
- Tech stack fundamentally incompatible

## How to Apply It

### Building Your ICP (First Time)

1. **Start with your best customers** (or closest design partners). What do they have in common?
2. **Look at your failed deals.** What did they have in common? Those become your no-go rules.
3. **Draft your IMP, ICP, IPP** using the tables above
4. **Pick 6 scoring criteria** — prioritize attributes that are **observable externally** (you need to score prospects before talking to them)
5. **Score your current pipeline** — does your ICP match where your deals are actually closing?

### Using Your ICP (Ongoing)

- **Before outreach:** Score the prospect. Below 3? Don't reach out.
- **During discovery:** Validate ICP assumptions. Ask questions that confirm or deny fit.
- **After calls:** Update your ICP based on what you learn. The ICP is a living document.
- **Monthly:** Review closed-won and closed-lost. Do your ICP criteria predict outcomes?

## Example

**Scenario:** A B2B project management tool targeting mid-market companies.

### IMP
| Attribute | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Industry | SaaS, professional services, digital agencies |
| Geography | US, UK, India (English-speaking markets) |
| Company Size | 50-500 employees |
| Tech Stack | Uses Slack, has existing PM tool frustration |
| Growth Stage | Series A through Series C |

### ICP
| Attribute | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Revenue | $5M-$50M ARR |
| Team Size | 10+ person product/engineering org |
| Growth Signals | Hiring PMs, expanding product lines |
| Tech Maturity | Uses modern dev stack, API-first mindset |
| Problem Evidence | Multiple PM tools in use, complaints about "tool sprawl" |
| Decision Structure | VP/Director level can make tool decisions |

### IPP
| Attribute | Definition |
|-----------|-----------|
| Title | VP of Product, Head of Engineering, Director of PMO |
| Authority | Can approve $10K-$50K/year tooling spend |
| Pain Ownership | Directly responsible for team delivery timelines |
| Career Incentive | Measured on team velocity and on-time delivery |

### Scoring
| Criterion | Description |
|-----------|------------|
| 1. Right vertical | SaaS, services, or agency |
| 2. Right size | 50-500 employees |
| 3. Growth signal | Hiring PMs or expanding teams |
| 4. Tool frustration | Evidence of multiple PM tools or complaints |
| 5. Right buyer | VP/Director accessible on LinkedIn |
| 6. No blockers | Not locked into competitor contract |

### No-Go Rules
- Under 20 employees (problem isn't painful enough)
- Recently signed 2-year contract with competitor
- No product/engineering function (consulting-only firms)

## Connection to Other Frameworks

- **Market Segments (Buyer's Pyramid):** Your ICP defines WHO. The pyramid tells you their readiness level. A Core ICP match in the "not thinking about it" tier still needs education.
- **Intent Signals:** Use intent signals to prioritize within your ICP — a Core match with strong intent signals is your highest-priority prospect.
- **Discovery Questions:** Use discovery calls to validate ICP fit. The Mom Test helps you confirm (or deny) assumptions about their problem.
- **Messaging Structure:** Your IPP drives persona-specific messaging — different titles care about different impacts.
- **Pipeline Scorecard:** ICP fit is a prerequisite for pipeline entry. Don't score commitment for accounts that aren't ICP.

## Anti-Patterns

| Anti-Pattern | What Happens | Fix |
|-------------|-------------|-----|
| **ICP too broad** | "We sell to any company with engineers" — you can't prioritize | Narrow until it feels uncomfortable. You can always expand later. |
| **ICP too narrow** | "We only sell to Series B fintech in NYC with 47 engineers" — no pipeline | Ensure your Core tier has at least 200+ addressable accounts |
| **ICP based on hope, not data** | You define the ICP you WANT, not the one that matches your actual wins | Start from closed deals and work backwards |
| **Static ICP** | You defined it 6 months ago and never updated | Review monthly — your ICP should evolve as you learn |
| **Ignoring no-go rules** | You pursue every warm lead even if they're clearly not a fit | Write down 3 hard disqualifiers and enforce them |
| **Scoring without action** | You score prospects but treat all scores the same | Different scores get different plays: Core = personalized, Broader = templated, Universe = inbound only |
